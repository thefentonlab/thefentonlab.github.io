# Join the lab

If you're interested in solving synthesis problems for new materials, get in touch.

## Graduate students

We are recruiting. Penn State admits to the Chemistry department rather than to individual
groups, so apply to the department and mention an interest in materials or inorganic
chemistry. Feel free to email me before you apply.

A good fit here looks like someone who wants to spend real time on synthesis and
characterization rather than handing samples to a facility and waiting. You'd be making
compounds nobody has made before, which means a lot of things won't work, and the
interesting part is figuring out why. Prior synthesis experience helps but isn't required.
Curiosity about crystal structures is more useful than a specific technique.

## Undergraduates

There is usually room for one or two new undergraduate researchers per semester. Penn State
students at any stage can email me. The strongest applications say something specific about
why this lab rather than a different one.

Undergraduates here run their own projects rather than washing glassware for a graduate
student. Expect roughly ten hours a week during the semester, and expect to still be
working on the same question a year later.

## Postdocs

Email me with a CV and a short description of what you'd want to work on. Funded positions
come and go, but I'm glad to talk about fellowship applications at any time.

## How to email

Send a few sentences about what you've worked on and what you want to learn. Mention one
paper from the group that you actually read and what struck you about it. Attach a CV.
That's enough.
